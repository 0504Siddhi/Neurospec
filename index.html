Reference Notes

How to Read This Tool

1. Channels & Sampling Rate (Hardware Complexity)

Channels are the scalp contact points, while sampling rate is how many times per second voltages are measured. Higher counts capture finer brain signal detail, but increase headset manufacturing cost and setup time.

2. Sensitivity (Patients Flagged for Referral)

Sensitivity is how many people who truly have cognitive impairment the tool flags, out of 100. A higher number means fewer missed patients.

3. Extra Referrals (Second-Stage Clinic Burden)

Extra referrals are healthy individuals inadvertently flagged for hospital follow-up, out of 100 people screened. Keeping this number low prevents secondary memory clinics from being overwhelmed by false alarms.

Clinical Context

Limitations & Clinical Boundaries

Limitations

"Based on one clinical dataset recorded with wet electrodes in a hospital. Real low-cost sensors may be noisier. Not clinically validated. Targets for sensitivity come from one clinician's rough view, not a standard. EEG's added value over free tests (MMSE, Mini-Cog, GPCOG) is not proven here."

  • Pre-screening only: This tool is engineered solely to assist community screening logistics and hardware budgeting. It does not provide definitive medical confirmation.
  • Electrode contact impedance: Laboratory studies often utilize conductive gels and supervised montage fitting. Field implementations using dry or semi-dry sensors may experience movement artifacts.
  • Comparative cost-benefit: Standard paper-and-pencil evaluations (e.g. Mini-Cog, MMSE) carry near-zero hardware costs. Any EEG hardware investment must justify itself through higher participation or reproducible referral yield.
