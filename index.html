Reference Notes

How to Read This Tool

1. Channels & Sampling Rate (Hardware Complexity)

Channels are scalp electrode contacts; sampling rate is how many times per second voltages are measured. In this model, sampling rate does not affect hardware chip cost. Higher channel counts capture broader spatial patterns, but sharply increase front-end chip costs and headset assembly complexity.

2. Sensitivity (Dementia Cases Detected)

Sensitivity is the percentage of individuals with dementia that the pre-screening tool successfully flags for secondary clinical evaluation. A sensitivity of 75% means 25 out of 100 individuals with dementia are missed during screening.

3. Extra Referrals (Second-Stage Clinic Burden)

Extra referrals are healthy seniors inadvertently referred to secondary clinics, expressed per 100 healthy individuals screened. Keeping this number low prevents secondary memory clinics and specialist doctors from being overwhelmed by false alarms.

Clinical Context

Limitations & Clinical Boundaries

Limitations

"Based on one clinical dataset (ds004504, 88 participants) recorded with wet electrodes in a hospital setting. Real low-cost dry sensors will be noisier. Not clinically validated. Targets for sensitivity reflect programmatic screening trade-offs, not clinical diagnostics. EEG's added value over zero-cost cognitive paper tests (MMSE, Mini-Cog, GPCOG) is not proven here."

  • Pre-screening only: This tool is engineered solely to assist community screening logistics and hardware budgeting. It does not provide definitive medical confirmation or replace comprehensive clinical workup.
  • Electrode contact impedance: Dataset ds004504 utilized conductive gel and supervised hospital placement. Field implementations using dry or semi-dry sensors will experience higher contact impedance and movement artifacts.
  • Comparative cost-benefit: Standard paper-and-pencil evaluations carry near-zero hardware costs. Any EEG hardware investment must justify itself through higher community compliance, objective automation, or reproducible referral yield.
